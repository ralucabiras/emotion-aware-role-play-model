\chapter{Evaluation and Results}
\label{chap5}

\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:model-evaluation-methodology}

The evaluation separates three kinds of evidence because they support different conclusions. First, speaker-independent IEMOCAP experiments address whether dialogue context improves text emotion classification (RQ1) and whether acoustic information improves a contextual text model (RQ2). Second, authored policy cases and application tests address whether uncertain predictions are translated into the specified bounded actions (RQ3) and whether the integrated system satisfies its declared requirements under reproducible developer-led verification (RQ4). Third, a small pilot provides supplementary feedback about the baseline rehearsal workflow. It does not evaluate emotion-aware adaptation because the required pilot tasks bypassed multimodal inference and affect pacing.

This hierarchy prevents benchmark accuracy, deterministic policy conformance, software verification, and user response from being collapsed into a single claim. IEMOCAP measures classification and probability quality on a controlled corpus; application tests establish behaviour only for their exercised paths and environments; and the pilot describes immediate experience only for the workflow participants encountered. Deployment readiness consequently remains conditional on target-environment validation.

\subsection{Evidence Scope and Decision Rules}
\label{subsec:evaluation-scope}

Each research question has a distinct evaluation unit. For RQ1, the unit is an IEMOCAP utterance paired with its causal dialogue history, and the admissible conclusion is a difference in benchmark text classification on identical held-out sessions. For RQ2, the unit is a matched text--audio utterance and the conclusion concerns complementary predictive information and calibration on the same benchmark. For RQ3, the unit is a versioned application decision within a controlled workflow; the tests may establish that the deterministic policy followed its specification, but not that a label was psychologically correct or that the action helped a person. For RQ4, the unit is the integrated application and its principal workflows, so several complementary checks are required across service, persistence, browser, accessibility, recovery, and timing boundaries.

The comparisons were chosen before interpreting the final pooled results. Utterance-only text is the RQ1 reference, and three-turn contextual text is the RQ2 reference. Predeclared equal fusion is the primary classification treatment; validation-fitted fusion answers the different question of whether operational probability quality can be recovered. The pilot is not promoted to a fifth evaluation level because its required tasks did not enter the multimodal path. It instead provides descriptive evidence about workflow completion and participant ratings for the deterministic baseline.

Evidence is reported at the narrowest defensible level. A passed test denotes observed conformance, not proof of correctness for all inputs. A bootstrap interval concerns the sampled dialogue clusters, not unseen populations. A zero-violation accessibility scan concerns automated rules and rendered states, not conformance certification. Similarly, a local latency distribution is a regression measurement rather than a production service-level result. These decision rules are applied consistently in the results and threats-to-validity sections.

\subsection{Benchmark Task and Speaker-Independent Protocol}
\label{subsec:evaluation-benchmark-splits}

The primary task uses the IEMOCAP categories anger, happiness, neutral, and sadness, with excitement merged into happiness and other source labels excluded \cite{busso2008iemocap}. It contains 5,531 matched text--audio utterances from 151 dialogues across five dyadic sessions. Text, audio, and fusion experiments use identical utterance identifiers and label order.

Five folds provide speaker-independent coverage. One complete session is held out for testing and another for validation in each fold; the remaining three sessions are used for training. Every session appears exactly once as test data, and both speakers in a dyad stay together. This design prevents a tested speaker's utterances from entering that fold's training set. Validation predictions select temperatures and, for the calibration-oriented fusion variant, fusion parameters. Only the held-out session supplies evaluation predictions.

The utterance-only and contextual text conditions use the same DeBERTa-v3-small architecture, folds, labels, seed, and optimisation settings. Their intended difference is the input: the contextual condition prepends the preceding three causal dialogue turns with speaker-relative markers. The paired audio condition uses wav2vec~2.0 on the 16~kHz waveform. The equal-weight fusion rule was fixed before inspecting combined test outcomes and is therefore the principal classification comparison. Fold-specific validation-fitted fusion is analysed separately as a probability-quality configuration.

Final application packages were trained on all eligible utterances after the cross-validation design and epoch counts were frozen. They have no independent test partition. Their training and smoke checks are integrity evidence rather than performance estimates; all model-performance claims below come from the five session-held-out experiments.

The separation of development roles is especially important for calibration. A temperature or fusion weight can appear effective if it is fitted and assessed on the same labels, even when the underlying classifier is unchanged. The fold-specific chain avoids that circularity by estimating every parameter from the validation session before applying it to the unseen test session. By contrast, the later global chain necessarily uses all available validation-role labels to prepare one deployable package and is reported only as a descriptive packaging check. This distinction allows the dissertation to discuss an operational configuration without presenting its label-reused diagnostic values as a second independent experiment. Engineering fixtures are separated for the same reason: their controlled probability vectors test policy branches, but they neither train the classifiers nor enter the IEMOCAP performance totals.

\subsection{Measures and Statistical Comparison}
\label{subsec:evaluation-measures}

Accuracy, macro F1, and weighted F1 are reported for classification. Macro F1 is primary because it weights the four classes equally despite unequal support. Probability quality is evaluated separately through expected calibration error (ECE), negative log-likelihood (NLL), and multiclass Brier score. ECE summarises the gap between confidence and accuracy within bins, but it is bin-dependent and incomplete; NLL and Brier score therefore provide complementary proper scoring rules \cite{guo2017calibration,kull2019dirichlet}. Temperature scaling is fitted to validation logits only:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{p}}=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}}{T}\right), \qquad T>0.
    \label{eq:evaluation-temperature}
\end{equation}
A scalar temperature changes probability concentration without changing the predicted class.

Because utterances from one conversation are dependent, comparisons use a paired percentile bootstrap over the 151 complete dialogues. In each of 10,000 replicates, dialogues are sampled with replacement and the same sampled clusters are applied to both systems before recomputing each aggregate metric. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles form the 95\% interval; the procedure uses seed 20260808. These intervals describe resampling uncertainty among the observed dialogues, not demographic, cultural, linguistic, device, or deployment generalisation.

Pooling the five non-overlapping test sessions produces one prediction for every eligible utterance and supplies the headline scores. Fold-level values are retained separately to show whether an aggregate result is driven by one session, but their unweighted mean is not interchangeable with a pooled nonlinear metric such as macro F1. No speaker-subgroup inference is attempted: IEMOCAP's ten actors provide too little support for such analysis. Per-class metrics are likewise descriptive unless their paired dialogue-bootstrap interval is reported.

The class effects were uneven. Happiness, neutral, and sadness F1 increased by 0.0882, 0.0338, and 0.1307, respectively, with dialogue-bootstrap intervals above zero. Anger F1 increased by 0.0198, but its interval of $[-0.0073,0.0449]$ included zero. The result therefore supports an aggregate contextual benefit, not an anger-specific improvement. The calibrated contextual model obtained ECE 0.0311 on the benchmark; this does not validate AffectLab's operational presentation thresholds on user-authored conversations.

The pattern is consistent with context resolving labels whose interpretation depends on an earlier exchange, while also showing that extra turns are not uniformly beneficial. In one held-out happiness example, an utterance-only neutral prediction changed to the correct class after context was added. Another held-out neutral example changed from a correct local decision to anger, illustrating contextual carry-over. A third neutral example assigned equal posterior mass of 0.4407 to happiness and neutral before the stored argmax selected happiness. These selected cases do not quantify failure frequency, but they show why a changed top label should not automatically be read as a decisive contextual interpretation.

The comparison also isolates only the combined effect of the contextual input representation. It does not determine whether the improvement came from additional lexical content, the identity-relative speaker tokens, sequential emotional consistency, or interaction structure. Component ablations would be required to attribute the gain among those mechanisms. Because both conditions share the same folds and training configuration, however, the aggregate paired difference remains a direct answer to the narrower question asked by RQ1.

An exploratory six-class task also improved from 0.5490 to 0.6222 macro F1, but only 40 of its 7,420 examples were mapped anxiety cases. The result is retained in the prepared Appendix~C source with the per-class and fold tables. It is secondary evidence and does not support a deployed anxiety-recognition claim.

Within this protocol, RQ1 is therefore answered positively: causal dialogue history improved aggregate text classification while architecture, data splits, and training settings were held constant. The experiment does not reveal whether the gain comes from additional lexical material, speaker markers, or discourse history, and it cannot show that a predicted category represents a user's internal emotional state.

\section{Speech, Fusion, and Calibration Results}
\label{sec:multimodal-results}

\subsection{Audio and Predeclared Fusion}
\label{subsec:equal-fusion-results}

The wav2vec~2.0 audio baseline obtained accuracy 0.6279, macro F1 0.6349, weighted F1 0.6229, and calibrated ECE 0.0468. It was weaker than contextual text overall but stronger for anger recall, suggesting that the branches contained complementary predictive information. This does not mean that the acoustic representation isolated prosody: it may also encode phonetic, lexical, speaker, and recording characteristics.

The principal fusion configuration averages the validation-calibrated text and audio posterior distributions with fixed weights of 0.5. Table~\ref{tab:equal-fusion-results} shows improvements over contextual text on every aggregate classification measure. Macro F1 increased by 0.0551, with a paired 95\% interval of 0.0407--0.0713; all 10,000 dialogue-bootstrap replicates favoured fusion for accuracy, macro F1, and weighted F1. Because the rule was predeclared rather than selected from fused test performance, it provides the headline RQ2 classification evidence.

The pooled class results reinforce that interpretation. Equal fusion reached F1 values of 0.8277 for anger, 0.8163 for happiness, 0.7014 for neutral, and 0.8080 for sadness. Neutral remained the hardest class. Inspection of matched errors showed why fusion is not uniformly corrective: a strong audio error could overturn a correct text decision, and agreement between two wrong branches could reinforce a shared error. These cases are explanatory examples rather than prevalence estimates; complete class and session results are preserved in the prepared Appendix~C source.

Matched predictions illustrate both directions. For one anger utterance, contextual text selected neutral while audio selected anger, and fusion recovered the annotated class. For a neutral utterance, text was correct but audio favoured anger strongly enough that fusion narrowly selected anger. In another neutral case, both modalities predicted anger and averaging reinforced the shared error. Late fusion can therefore exploit complementary mistakes but cannot correct correlated ones, and a fixed average can allow an overconfident branch to dominate a better counterpart.

Session-level macro F1 preserved the aggregate ordering. Context exceeded utterance-only text in all five held-out sessions, and equal fusion was the strongest classifier in every session. Audio varied most, with fold scores from 0.5870 to 0.6974 and a descriptive standard deviation of 0.0386; contextual text and equal fusion each had a standard deviation of about 0.0227. These five dyads reveal sensitivity within the corpus but are too few to estimate population speaker variability.

\subsection{Classification--Calibration Trade-off}
\label{subsec:fusion-calibration-results}

Equal averaging achieved the strongest classification but ECE rose to 0.1619. Separately calibrated modalities therefore did not remain calibrated after their probabilities were averaged. This matters operationally because AffectLab consumes confidence when selecting whether to preserve baseline pacing, offer a choice, or permit a bounded acknowledgement.

Fold-specific validation fitting selected text weights between 0.54 and 0.63 and fitted a final temperature. As Table~\ref{tab:calibration-tradeoff} shows, this reduced ECE to 0.0283 while retaining accuracy 0.7717 and macro F1 0.7758. Its NLL and Brier score were also the lowest among the independently evaluated configurations. The approximately one-point macro-F1 reduction relative to equal fusion is consequently an explicit trade-off, not an inconsistency between results.

Figure~\ref{fig:model-results} places the main classification and calibration results on common scales. The proper scores qualify an ECE-only interpretation: equal fusion was under-confident, yet its NLL and Brier score improved over both unimodal branches. Validation-fitted fusion then improved all three probability-quality measures. Classification quality and probability quality must therefore remain separate claims.

The apparently counter-intuitive equal-fusion result follows from the metrics measuring different properties. ECE compares average confidence with empirical accuracy inside bins, whereas NLL and Brier score use the complete predicted distribution. Equal fusion often placed useful probability mass on the correct class while remaining systematically under-confident in its populated mid-confidence bins. Validation-derived weighting and temperature scaling brought maximum confidence closer to observed accuracy without preserving every classification decision. For an application whose policy consumes confidence, the better-calibrated configuration is therefore preferable even though the fixed average has the highest F1.

The application requires a single chain for models trained on all available data. A global fit selected text/audio weights of 0.59/0.41 and a final temperature of 0.5989. Its descriptive pooled values were macro F1 0.7776 and ECE 0.0242, but labels used in calibration fitting recur in the reporting pool through predictions from different fold models. These are therefore label-reused packaging diagnostics, not additional held-out evidence. The equal-weight result remains the classification headline, while fold-specific validation fitting supplies the independent calibration evidence.

The closest published protocol-compatible result is Li et al.'s four-class, five-session IEMOCAP study, which likewise reports text outperforming audio and multimodal classification outperforming both \cite{li2022contextaware}. AffectLab's equal fusion reached 78.32\% accuracy compared with their 80.36\% multimodal value. This is descriptive placement rather than a head-to-head ranking: their scores average folds, their validation allocation and architectures differ, and their text model uses future as well as preceding context. Six-class and random utterance-split studies are not treated as comparable.

RQ2 is thus answered positively within IEMOCAP: acoustic information improved classification over contextual text on matched held-out examples. The operational answer must include the calibration trade-off, however. Neither benchmark calibration nor the global packaging chain guarantees reliable confidence after domain shift \cite{ovadia2019shift}.

\subsection{Bounded Policy Behaviour}
\label{subsec:policy-case-results}

Five authored enhanced-dialogue trajectories covered workload, boundary, and relationship scenarios. All followed their expected controller stages, including non-confirmation and unresolved endings. The cases show that completion requires scenario-specific evidence rather than a generic success phrase. They do not establish that the resulting dialogue is more realistic, persuasive, or educational.

Twelve human-authored judgements tested the lexical evidence extractor. The false positive arose when reported speech containing ``I need'' was attributed to the current speaker; an indirect refusal and the synonym ``shift'' produced the two false negatives. Keeping these errors visible demonstrates the limitations of deterministic extraction and motivates replaying the source utterance alongside derived feedback. The counts are diagnostics, not a held-out accuracy rate.

Seven affect cases covered confident agreement, modality disagreement, low confidence, missing audio, unavailable inference, disabled adaptation, and explicit preference. All produced the declared action: confident agreement permitted a brief bounded acknowledgement; disagreement and low confidence offered a pacing choice; failures and disabled adaptation preserved the baseline; and an explicit user preference took precedence. Separate tests verify that safety, completion, study eligibility, and required-task rules override adaptation. The classifier supplies evidence, while deterministic dialogue and safety policies retain authority.

Implementation-authored cognitive and support-routing cases similarly verify only stable rule execution and precedence. They do not validate constructs such as intent, cause, readiness, resistance, or cognitive distortion, nor do they show that a selected strategy is helpful or harmless. This distinction bounds the RQ3 conclusion: AffectLab demonstrably maps uncertain or unavailable evidence into auditable actions and fallbacks under tested conditions, but it does not establish the truth of an inferred state or the benefit of adapting to it.

Persistence and replay make the policy observable rather than merely repeatable in memory. Each decision is bound to an exchange version and stores the modality evidence, confidence state, selected action, and fallback reason needed to reconstruct the outcome. Optimistic concurrency rejects stale updates, preventing a delayed inference result from silently attaching to a newer turn. Replay presents the original utterance beside derived evidence so that a user can inspect the basis of feedback, while branching creates a new history rather than rewriting the original. These mechanisms support explainability as traceability of system action; they do not make the underlying inference inherently correct.

\subsection{Integrated Quality and Local Responsiveness}
\label{subsec:automated-system-results}

The final default backend run collected 258 cases: 245 passed and 13 explicitly skipped because a real-MongoDB URI was absent. The skips belong to one declared environment-dependent integration module and are not counted as passes. Ruff, the production frontend build, and frontend linting passed. A focused browser run expanded 38 logical cases across desktop Chromium and a Pixel~7 profile, producing 76 passes. Twenty axe-core scans covered ten interface states in both profiles and detected no violations under the selected automated WCAG~2.0/2.1 A/AA rule tags. This is evidence about the exercised DOM states, not accessibility certification.

The browser cases covered preparation, active practice, restored reflection, voice-text review, uncertain pacing, stored provenance, replay, branch comparison, and action-card views. They also asserted keyboard interaction, focus behaviour, status announcements, preservation of unsent input after failure, and recovery controls. Testing both projects checks responsive layout at the declared viewports, but it does not sample the wider device, browser, zoom, or assistive-technology space.

Two separately recorded smoke journeys used the compiled frontend, an actual FastAPI process, and disposable MongoDB~8.0.1. They covered account access, consent and study flow, required-task persistence, researcher export, enhanced dialogue, failure recovery, replay, branching, comparison, and action-card persistence. State remained correct across two backend restarts. Trained inference, transcription, and generation providers were disabled, so the journeys establish integration and recovery for the deterministic configuration rather than live-provider behaviour.

The relationship between the default and smoke evidence is complementary. The fast default suite gives broad deterministic coverage but substitutes the memory repository; the two smoke journeys provide narrower end-to-end coverage with real process and database boundaries. The 13 skipped MongoDB tests make this division explicit instead of treating unavailable infrastructure as success. Neither layer independently supports a production claim, but together they show that the application contract survives both component-level variation and the recorded database-backed journeys.

The local regression benchmark exercised FastAPI's in-process HTTP boundary with authentication, validation, services, serialisation, and the memory repository. All five predeclared budgets passed. The deterministic conversation turn had median 4.659~ms and nearest-rank P95 5.836~ms over 100 sequential samples. These numbers characterise locally controlled application overhead only; they exclude network transit, MongoDB, trained inference, provider latency, and concurrent clients.

Together, the authored, automated, browser, persistence, recovery, and timing evidence answers RQ4 for the tested offline deterministic core: the implemented paths conform to their declared functional and non-functional requirements and are suitable for controlled deployment after environment-specific validation. Production claims require staged tests with intended models and providers, real network and database infrastructure, expected concurrency, backup recovery, observability, and independent security assessment.

\section{Supplementary Pilot Results}
\label{subsec:participant-results}

The single-arm pilot examined only the baseline text rehearsal with deterministic dialogue and feedback. Every required task was ineligible for multimodal inference and affect-aware pacing. The results below therefore cannot establish whether emotion-aware adaptation is acceptable, accurate, useful, beneficial, or harmful.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:evaluation-limitations}

\paragraph{Model and statistical validity.}
IEMOCAP contains ten actors in acted or improvised English dialogue. Session-held-out folds prevent identity leakage for the tested speaker, but five dyads cannot represent demographic, cultural, linguistic, device, microphone, disability, or deployment diversity. Dialogue bootstrapping respects within-conversation dependence among the 151 observed dialogues but cannot create missing populations. One primary training seed leaves optimisation variance incompletely characterised, and small numerical variation can arise across GPU executions. Commit, configuration, prediction, and metric provenance is therefore necessary; provenance for private final model files remains incomplete.

The final full-data packages have no independent test set, and their pooled packaging diagnostics reuse labels across validation and reporting roles. They must not replace the held-out fold evidence. ECE depends on binning and may deteriorate under distribution shift; even favourable IEMOCAP calibration does not validate confidence in a deployment population. The 0.55 and 0.75 policy bands are operational presentation settings rather than psychological boundaries.

\paragraph{Construct and external validity.}
IEMOCAP labels are annotations of expressive categories, not direct observations of internal emotion. Merging excitement with happiness and mapping rare fear cases to exploratory anxiety simplify already contested constructs. Scores must not be interpreted as diagnosis, emotional truth, or equitable performance. The six-class anxiety result is especially fragile because it contains only 40 mapped examples.

Application features are operational constructs as well. Lexical rules can locate phrases such as explicit requests or refusals in authored cases, but they do not measure general communication ability, intent, cause, readiness, resistance, cognition, or transfer to a real conversation. No clinician, validated questionnaire, independent annotation study, or held-out corpus establishes the cognitive fields as psychological measures. Deterministic feedback improves auditability, not construct validity. Negation, quotation, indirect language, dialect, culture, and multiple simultaneous requests remain likely failure sources.

\paragraph{Application and deployment validity.}
Engineering tests use controlled distributions, synthetic accounts, deterministic templates, and selected browser states. They do not establish trained-model latency, real-microphone robustness, live-provider variability, concurrent capacity, production security, backup operations, long-duration reliability, or absence of harm. Zero automated accessibility violations is narrower than WCAG conformance and does not substitute for screen-reader or participatory testing. The in-process benchmark excludes the components most sensitive to deployment infrastructure. A controlled deployment therefore requires target-population model validation, model and provider integration tests, staged load testing, recovery exercises, monitoring, and independent security and accessibility review.

\paragraph{Pilot validity.}
The pilot is small, single-arm, uncontrolled, incomplete in places, and based on study-specific ordinal measures. Most importantly, required tasks bypassed the multimodal and affect-aware path, so the pilot supplies no human evidence for the dissertation's adaptation mechanism. Immediate confidence change may reflect repetition, demand characteristics, or selection effects and cannot establish sustained learning or real-world transfer. The pilot is therefore retained only as contextual evidence that some participants completed and positively rated the baseline workflow.

In summary, the evaluation supports four bounded conclusions: causal context improved four-class text classification on session-held-out IEMOCAP; audio added complementary benchmark information but introduced a calibration trade-off under equal fusion; deterministic policies implemented the declared uncertainty, preference, fallback, and safety precedence; and the integrated offline core passed its scoped verification. Chapter~\ref{chap6} draws the final research conclusions without extending them beyond these boundaries.
