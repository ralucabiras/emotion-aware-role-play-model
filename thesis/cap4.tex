\chapter{Implementation}
\label{chap:implementation}

\section{Technology and Application Overview}
\label{sec:implementation-overview}
\label{sec:technology-stack}

AffectLab is implemented as a modular web application whose trained models advise, but do not control, the rehearsal workflow. A React and TypeScript client presents preparation, role-play, feedback, and reflection views. A Python and FastAPI backend owns authentication, dialogue state, safety decisions, feedback evidence, and persistence in MongoDB. Contextual text and speech classifiers can add calibrated affect estimates, while optional provider services can transcribe audio or realise an authorised response in natural language. Each optional component is behind a narrow interface, so the text-based deterministic workflow remains available when model artefacts, credentials, or network services are absent.

This separation is also reflected in deployment. The browser submits typed requests and replaces its local view with the version committed by the backend; it does not calculate authoritative transitions or scores. Backend routes validate transport objects, application services implement policy, and a repository interface supplies either MongoDB persistence or an in-memory substitute for isolated demonstrations. Private datasets, model weights, credentials, and transient audio remain outside version-controlled application code. Dependency sets separate the core web service from the larger local-inference runtime, and containers package the compiled client, API, and database without embedding private artefacts.

Typed boundaries carry the distinctions through the application. A conversation turn can retain the emotion distribution available at that instant, the selected support or character action, the response source, and the pacing decision, while a role-play decision additionally retains its policy version and state snapshots. Absence is represented explicitly rather than as a zero-valued observation. This permits later replay and evaluation to distinguish deterministic, provider-backed, model-assisted, and failed paths without reconstructing them from a transcript.

The implementation is text-first and failure-tolerant. Dialogue progression, evidence collection, crisis interruption, feedback, replay, and session management do not require a graphics processor or external provider. Probabilistic outputs pass through a bounded pacing policy. A language model may phrase a response or feedback item, but it cannot choose an action, declare success, create evidence, or override safety. These authority boundaries organise the mechanisms below.

\section{Emotion-Recognition Pipeline}
\label{sec:emotion-recognition-pipeline}

The model pipeline converts a private IEMOCAP release into separate text and speech classifiers and then packages compatible full-data artefacts for optional application inference. Preprocessing joins annotations, transcripts, and sentence-level waveforms by utterance identifier, rejects duplicates or missing inputs, and records source and manifest digests. The principal task maps anger, happiness plus excitement, neutrality, and sadness to a fixed four-class order, producing 5,531 matched text--audio utterances. Licensed media and row-level derived data are kept outside the repository. An exploratory six-class mapping is retained only as a secondary research configuration and is not used by the deployed multimodal service.

Figure~\ref{fig:emotion-pipeline} distinguishes evaluation from deployment packaging. Five speaker-independent folds are constructed from complete IEMOCAP sessions: one session is held out for testing, one for validation, and three for training. Because each actor occurs in one session, preprocessing can verify that speakers do not cross split boundaries. These folds provide the evidence reported in Chapter~\ref{chap5}. Only after the experimental configuration is frozen are final text and audio models trained on all eligible examples for application use; their training outputs are not treated as a new held-out result.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\resizebox{0.86\textwidth}{!}{\input{figures/emotion_pipeline}}
\caption{Emotion-recognition evaluation and deployment pipeline. Full-data packaging creates application artefacts, not additional held-out evidence.}
\label{fig:emotion-pipeline}
\end{figure}

The contextual text classifier fine-tunes \texttt{microsoft/deberta-v3-small}, a compact DeBERTaV3 encoder \cite{he2021debertav3}. Its utterance-only configuration receives the target transcript, whereas the contextual configuration prepends at most three earlier turns from the same dialogue. Each earlier turn is marked as spoken by the same or other speaker relative to the target, and the target receives its own marker. Future turns, emotion annotations, and dialogue outcomes are excluded. The backend reconstructs the same causal format from the stored conversation, avoiding a mismatch between training and inference context.
\label{subsec:contextual-text-emotion-model}

The acoustic classifier fine-tunes \texttt{facebook/wav2vec2-base} on raw waveforms \cite{baevski2020wav2vec}. Audio is converted to mono, resampled to 16~kHz, limited to 20 seconds, and dynamically padded within a batch. The feature encoder is frozen while the transformer and classification head are trained. Text and audio artefacts share the same label order, and their prediction files must have identical utterance identifiers before fusion is permitted.
\label{subsec:speech-emotion-model}

Both classifiers are selected by validation macro F1 rather than test performance. Text fine-tuning uses cross-entropy, a small learning rate, warm-up, weight decay, and early stopping; speech fine-tuning adds gradient accumulation and checkpointing to fit the raw-waveform model within available memory. Each fold records its seed, session assignment, label order, training history, environment, validation calibration, and row-level test distributions. The fusion program verifies these identifiers and reconstructs the saved single-modality metrics before combining probabilities, preventing a result from being produced from mismatched rows or class orders.

Decision-level late fusion keeps the modalities independently trainable and inspectable \cite{baltrusaitis2019multimodal}. For text logits $z_t$ and audio logits $z_a$, validation-fitted temperatures first produce $p_t=\operatorname{softmax}(z_t/T_t)$ and $p_a=\operatorname{softmax}(z_a/T_a)$. The distributions are then combined as

\begin{equation}
 p_m = w p_t + (1-w)p_a,
 \label{eq:implementation-late-fusion}
\end{equation}

with a predeclared $w=0.5$ for the principal research comparison. A separate deployment path chooses $w$ and a final temperature from validation-role predictions only. Temperature scaling changes probability concentration without changing the largest logit \cite{guo2017calibration}; classification and calibration are consequently evaluated as separate properties. The packaged global parameters are operational settings rather than independent performance evidence because their pooled fitting procedure reuses utterance labels across rotating fold roles.
\label{subsec:multimodal-late-fusion}
\label{subsec:confidence-calibration-uncertainty}

At inference, the backend returns complete calibrated distributions, selected labels, confidence, modality agreement, and separate queue and execution times. Confidence below 0.55 is presented as low, from 0.55 to below 0.75 as moderate, and at least 0.75 as high. These are operational display and policy thresholds, not psychological boundaries. Missing audio, disagreement, invalid distributions, or unavailable models remain explicit; they are never converted into a stronger estimate. Calibration can also deteriorate under shifts in speaker, language, device, and interaction style \cite{ovadia2019shift}, so the application treats the distributions as uncertain evidence rather than descriptions of internal state.

The private inference package contains the two models, their tokenizer or feature extractor, the fixed label order, input limits, and the complete calibration chain. A provenance manifest binds these files to source and configuration hashes without publishing licensed weights. Models load lazily and run behind an asynchronous lock to bound memory contention. Multimodal inference is disabled by default and failure returns the exchange to the deterministic text path.
\label{subsec:model-artefacts-provenance}
\label{subsec:reproducibility-provenance}

\section{Backend and Request Processing}
\label{sec:backend-application}

The FastAPI backend is the authoritative coordination layer. API routes and Pydantic schemas constrain inputs and outputs, application services coordinate authentication, conversation, model, and provider functions, and repositories encapsulate mutable state. This layering keeps transport logic separate from dialogue policy and permits deterministic or trained implementations to satisfy the same interfaces. Session documents retain ordered turns, role-play snapshots, evidence, policy decisions, feedback, model metadata, version, and expiry time; response schemas expose only what each interface requires.
\label{subsec:backend-service-architecture}
\label{subsec:backend-domain-models}

Authentication and routine account operations use the same service boundary but are not part of the research contribution. Passwords and verification or recovery tokens are stored only as adaptive hashes or digests. Short-lived access tokens authorise API requests, while rotated refresh tokens use HTTP-only cookies. More importantly for the rehearsal data, every session lookup includes both the session and authenticated owner identifiers. Each write also supplies an expected monotonically increasing version and a request identifier; stale or duplicate submissions return a conflict rather than silently overwriting a committed turn. MongoDB provides durable storage and expiry indexes, while the in-memory repository implements the same contract for controlled local use.
\label{subsec:backend-authentication}
\label{subsec:backend-session-persistence}

Request schemas enforce message, title, reflection, and encoded-audio limits before service execution. Enhanced exchanges must pair their expected version with a unique request identifier, and received probability vectors are accepted only when all values are finite, bounded, normalised, and consistent with the declared leading label. The backend derives agreement and confidence bands again instead of trusting client-authored summaries. These checks keep a malformed enhancement from contaminating the authoritative session even when ordinary text processing can continue.

Figure~\ref{fig:request-sequence} shows the processing order for a conversation exchange. After validation, authentication, ownership, duplication, and version checks, a local crisis-language detector runs before affect inference or response generation. A safety match interrupts the rehearsal through fixed wording and skips optional processing. Otherwise, eligible requests may obtain a multimodal estimate; failure is recorded as unavailability and does not abort the message. The service then appends the user turn, advances the deterministic controller, records evidence, applies any permitted pacing action, realises the authorised reply, and persists the complete transition atomically. The response contains the committed version and server-owned state so the browser does not need to infer what succeeded.
\label{subsec:backend-conversation-processing}
\label{subsec:safety-precedence}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\resizebox{0.84\textwidth}{!}{\input{figures/request_sequence}}
\caption{Version-bound conversation request with safety precedence and failure-tolerant optional processing.}
\label{fig:request-sequence}

Provider-backed response realisation follows the same authority rule. It receives the selected support strategy or role-play action and must return a typed, short response consistent with that action. Empty, refused, malformed, unsafe, or contradictory output is discarded in favour of deterministic wording. A small rule-based analyser can expose tentative lexical intent, possible patterns, and authored support priorities outside active role-play, but these values are rule matches rather than learned probabilities or validated psychological measurements. During role-play, neither these heuristics nor provider output may advance the scenario, create evidence, or determine success.
\label{subsec:backend-fallbacks}
\label{subsec:cognitive-support-routing}

\section{Role-Play and Affect-Adaptation Engine}
\label{sec:roleplay-adaptation}

A scenario is represented by its participant objective, character, opening line, supported observable skills, difficulty behaviour, completion conditions, and maximum turn count. Standard scenarios cover workload prioritisation, maintaining a boundary, expressing a relationship need, workplace feedback, deadline negotiation, and shared responsibilities; the first three form the fixed pilot sequence. Custom scenarios reuse only supported features and present any prepared objection as an editable rehearsal prompt, not as a prediction of another person.
\label{subsec:scenario-representation}
\label{subsec:frontend-preparation}

The enhanced workload, boundary, and relationship scenarios are implemented as versioned state graphs. Workload practice moves from describing the problem through acknowledging constraints to a workable plan. Boundary practice requires a clear refusal to survive authored pressure before respectful closure. Relationship practice requires a specific need, acknowledgement of perspective, and a practical request. A transition occurs only when the evidence required at the current stage is present; contradictory or unsupported input leaves the controller in place. Attempts can also end at the turn limit, by manual finish, or through safety interruption, and each decision stores its before-and-after snapshot so a restored session does not reconstruct state from prose.
\label{subsec:dialogue-transitions}

Difficulty controls authored resistance and cooperation rather than changing what a detected feature means. Eligible additional practice can also select a cooperative, rushed, or sceptical character profile, each backed by a versioned policy; profiles are excluded from required pilot tasks. Every processed rehearsal turn records the chosen action, reason codes, linked user and assistant turns, supporting evidence identifiers, generation source, and scenario, policy, and scoring versions. Completion reasons remain distinct, allowing the same persisted representation to support recovery, audit, and fair comparison.

Evidence extraction records bounded language features such as a concrete request, specific detail, I-statement, non-blaming wording, maintained boundary, acknowledgement, or feasible proposal. Each item links to its source turn, and features may accumulate only where the scenario policy permits. The rules account for selected negations and contradictions but cannot cover every indirect, sarcastic, multilingual, or complex expression. A match therefore means that an authored rule observed a feature in this rehearsal; it is not proof of intent, competence, or probable real-world success.
\label{subsec:communication-evidence}

The affect-pacing policy in Figure~\ref{fig:affect-policy} is applied only after the controller has selected an action. Safety, dialogue state, and explicit participant preference take precedence. Automatic adaptation is disabled by default and is limited to active, enhanced, non-required rehearsals. Required pilot tasks, retries associated with those tasks, paused or completed attempts, and unsupported scenarios remain on the baseline path. The participant can independently request unchanged, gentler, or more challenging pacing, so the control remains usable without voice.

When automatic adaptation is eligible, missing audio, inference failure, or an unsupported category produces no addition. Low confidence in any component or disagreement between fused, text, and audio leaders produces a neutral pacing choice. Only when all three labels agree and meet the low-confidence boundary can anger or sadness add the fixed acknowledgement ``I hear you.''; happiness or neutrality adds nothing. The policy never translates a label into anxiety, resistance, readiness, or a clinical state, and it cannot change stage, evidence, success conditions, or feedback scores. The stored decision includes the selected action, reason, preference, model and policy versions, timing, and distributions, allowing the exact behaviour to be explained after reload.
\label{subsec:affect-pacing}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\resizebox{0.64\textwidth}{!}{\input{figures/affect_policy}}
\caption{Bounded affect-aware pacing. Every terminal action preserves the authoritative dialogue state and evidence.}
\label{fig:affect-policy}
\end{figure}

Response wording begins with the action and fallback supplied by the deterministic controller. If generation is enabled, the provider must repeat the required action, stay in character, avoid coaching language, and preserve any concrete negotiated constraint. Parsing failure, action mismatch, disallowed content, or loss of a material fact restores the fallback. Branches also use deterministic wording so their differences arise from the participant's alternative response rather than uncontrolled regeneration. Safety uses an independent local detector and fixed interruption path before either mechanism; it is an engineering safeguard, not a validated clinical risk assessment.
\label{subsec:response-realisation}

\section{Feedback and Frontend Workflow}
\label{sec:feedback-reflection}
\label{sec:frontend-application}

Feedback is calculated from the evidence and decisions stored during the rehearsal rather than from a new model judgement. Scenario-specific metrics refer to supported features and their source turns; deterministic templates convert them into observed strengths and next-attempt suggestions. The completion reason remains separate, so reaching a turn limit is not relabelled as success and a safety interruption is not scored as a skill failure. Optional provider phrasing may reword an already calculated item but cannot alter measurements or invent observations. Comparisons with earlier attempts are made only under the same scoring version and are described as differences in observed rehearsal language.
\label{subsec:evidence-feedback}

Replay is a read-only projection of the recorded exchanges, state transitions, generation source, linked evidence, and saved pacing decision. Rewind restores a preceding stored snapshot only when the latest user--assistant pair can be removed safely. Branching instead creates a copy-on-write child from a validated pre-turn snapshot; copied context remains distinguishable from the new continuation and is not counted again in feedback. The original attempt is unchanged, and comparison presents both continuations without ranking either as globally better. A personal takeaway and editable action card remain user-authored reflection rather than evaluation evidence.
\label{subsec:conversation-replay}
\label{subsec:rewind-branching}
\label{subsec:takeaways-action-cards}

Measurement boundaries prevent later reflection from entering rehearsal feedback. Missing legacy decisions or model output are displayed as gaps rather than regenerated. A branch validates source ownership, version, policy compatibility, and the selected pre-turn snapshot, then stores its lineage and copied-turn boundary. Action-card suggestions reuse only eligible participant turns inside the measurement window; safety-interrupted content and copied branch context are excluded, and every field remains editable before saving.

The React interface follows the same lifecycle: preparation, active rehearsal, feedback, replay or branching, and an action card. The representative role-play view in Figure~\ref{fig:app-roleplay} combines the transcript and composer with scenario progress and optional voice controls. The browser includes the displayed session version and a fresh request identifier, clears a draft only after confirmation, and offers reload after a conflict while preserving unsent text. Semantic controls, visible focus, live status regions, reduced-motion rules, and responsive layouts support keyboard, desktop, and mobile use.
\label{subsec:frontend-navigation}
\label{subsec:frontend-roleplay-workspace}
\label{subsec:frontend-feedback-interfaces}
\label{subsec:frontend-testing}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth,height=0.22\textheight,keepaspectratio]{figures/app-roleplay.png}
\caption{Representative role-play workspace with transcript, scenario state, message composer, and optional voice interaction.}

Voice input is opt-in for each exchange. The browser requests microphone access only after an explicit action, records at most 20 seconds, converts the signal to mono 16~kHz PCM WAV, and keeps it in component memory. A transcription result enters the composer for review rather than becoming a turn automatically. The reviewed text and optional audio are then sent together so prediction, pacing, and stored wording refer to one exchange. The backend validates the audio and processes it in memory; raw recordings are not placed in sessions or research exports.
\label{subsec:frontend-voice}
\label{subsec:audio-conversation-data}

The interface distinguishes the trained multimodal distribution from a separate deterministic lexical display. When multimodal output exists, it shows fused confidence, modality leaders, agreement, the full distribution, timings, and an audio-not-stored notice. Disagreement and unavailable inference remain visible. Reloading uses the persisted decision rather than rerunning the model, preventing a later estimate from being presented as the reason for an earlier response.
\label{subsec:frontend-affect-presentation}

Optional research support is limited to a versioned pilot workflow: explicit consent and eligibility, three ordered required tasks, immediate questionnaires, pseudonymous progress records, and a text-free export. Required tasks deliberately bypass multimodal inference and affect-aware pacing. The workflow therefore supports collection for the baseline deterministic rehearsal only; its implementation does not make the pilot evidence about adaptation. This distinction is carried into the evaluation in Chapter~\ref{chap5}.
\label{sec:research-workflow-implementation}

\section{Security, Persistence, and Deployment}
\label{sec:security-privacy-data-protection}
\label{sec:deployment-configuration}

Security controls concentrate on ownership, minimisation, and explicit failure boundaries. MongoDB queries bind session identifiers to their owner; optimistic versions prevent lost updates; production configuration rejects weak signing secrets, wildcard origins or hosts, disabled HTTPS enforcement, and disabled rate limiting. Conversation text persists because continuation, replay, and evidence links require it, but raw audio is transient. Research projections exclude identity, transcripts, free-text feedback, custom wording, and audio and use a retention schedule separate from ordinary sessions. Account deletion cascades through owned operational records, while study withdrawal removes live research records without pretending that irreversibly de-identified frozen exports can still be linked.

Operational and research records have different purposes and expiry fields. The richer account-owned session supports continuation and user-controlled deletion; the pseudonymous study projection carries protocol versions, task classification, questionnaire values, numeric feedback, and event counts without copying conversation content. Freezing creates a checksum-bound de-identified export and closes further protocol mutations. This lifecycle keeps pilot administration from silently expanding the information retained for ordinary practice.

The integrated deployment uses a compiled frontend, FastAPI service, and MongoDB with health checks. Readiness verifies configuration, database access, and required indexes; missing optional transcription or model capability is reported separately and does not make the text-first application unavailable. Expected authentication, validation, rate-limit, provider, model, and concurrency failures become bounded client responses. Authority failures---missing ownership, stale state, invalid consent, or a frozen protocol---fail closed, whereas enhancement failures return to deterministic operation.
\label{subsec:health-failure-handling}

\label{sec:implementation-verification}
\label{subsec:performance-testing}

Verification evidence is reported once in Chapter~\ref{chap5}. Unit, service, browser, and full-stack checks exercise the compiled client and real database while external providers and trained inference remain disabled; performance therefore describes only that controlled local path. Reproduction additionally requires the source revision, locked dependencies, versioned configurations, and private artefact hashes.
