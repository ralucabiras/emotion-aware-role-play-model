\section*{Abstract} 
Practising difficult interpersonal conversations can be inaccessible, inconsistent, or uncomfortable, while conventional conversational systems often ignore context, vocal cues, and uncertainty. This dissertation presents AffectLab, a non-clinical application for adaptive communication rehearsal that combines contextual text and speech emotion recognition with deterministic dialogue, feedback, safety, and fallback policies. Emotion models were evaluated on five speaker-independent IEMOCAP folds using classification, calibration, and dialogue-level bootstrap measures; the integrated application was assessed through authored cases, automated and full-stack tests, accessibility checks, restart recovery, and local latency measurements. Three-turn context increased text macro F1 from 0.6651 to 0.7332. Equal-weight text--audio fusion reached 0.7883 macro F1 and 0.7832 accuracy, improving macro F1 over contextual text by 0.0551 (95\% interval 0.0407--0.0713), although its ECE of 0.1619 exposed a calibration trade-off. The application passed 245 backend tests, 76 browser-profile executions of 38 logical cases, production frontend checks, and two database-backed restart journeys; deterministic conversation turns had a 5.836~ms 95th-percentile local latency. In a supplementary single-arm pilot, 12 of 15 enrollees completed the protocol, while realism and feedback usefulness each had a median rating of 5/7. Required pilot tasks bypassed multimodal inference and affect-aware pacing, so these ratings describe only the baseline rehearsal workflow and provide no evidence about the acceptability or benefit of emotion-aware adaptation. The small, uncontrolled pilot also cannot establish causal benefit or real-world transfer. Within these limits, AffectLab demonstrates an implementation-complete, uncertainty-aware system evaluated under a documented configuration. Independent model reproduction remains conditional on authorised IEMOCAP access and completion of the archived final-model hashes, while suitability for controlled deployment depends on target-environment validation.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} affective computing; emotion recognition; multimodal fusion; conversational context; difficult-conversation rehearsal; human--computer interaction.
