\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{chap6}

\section{Conclusions for the Research Questions}
\label{sec:conclusion-research-questions}

This dissertation asked whether conversational context and speech provide useful affective evidence, how such evidence can be used without giving a classifier authority over a role-play, and whether the resulting application meets its declared engineering requirements. The evaluation in Chapter~\ref{chap5} supports the following bounded answers.

\paragraph{RQ1: conversational context.}
\label{subsec:conclusion-rq1}
Preceding turns improved text classification under the four-class, speaker-independent IEMOCAP protocol. Matched conditions and the paired dialogue comparison support a contextual-input gain in this experiment, while leaving training-seed variability and transfer unresolved. The exploratory six-class result does not support an anxiety-recognition claim (Section~\ref{sec:context-text-results}).

\paragraph{RQ2: multimodal complementarity and calibration.}
\label{subsec:conclusion-rq2}
Audio complemented contextual text despite being the weaker individual modality. Equal fusion produced the strongest classification, while validation-fitted fusion recovered better calibration with most of the gain. RQ2 therefore requires separate classification and probability-quality conclusions (Section~\ref{sec:multimodal-results}).

\paragraph{RQ3: bounded use of affective evidence.}
\label{subsec:conclusion-rq3}
Authored cases and integrated journeys supported the declared authority boundaries and reversible pacing policy. This establishes explainable behaviour under tested conditions, leaving prediction correctness for a particular user and adaptation benefit unestablished. Section~\ref{subsec:policy-case-results} gives the supporting evidence.

\paragraph{RQ4: integrated application quality.}
\label{subsec:conclusion-rq4}
The offline deterministic configuration conformed on the exercised functional, recovery, interface, and timing paths. This supports implementation completeness within the verification scope in Section~\ref{sec:engineering-evaluation}. The unevaluated deployment conditions in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-limitations} remain prerequisites for broader readiness claims.

\section{Contributions and Material Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion-contributions}

The three contributions stated in Section~\ref{sec:contributions} are now supported within their declared scope: an \textbf{empirical} comparison of context, fusion, and calibration; an \textbf{architectural} demonstration of bounded affect-aware rehearsal with evidence-linked feedback and alternative continuations; and a \textbf{methodological} separation of model, policy, application, performance, and supplementary user evidence. Versioned decisions and replayable state make the implemented integration inspectable.

The main limitations are corpus-specific inference, a single primary training seed, and limited coverage of scenario and lexical rules. Neither emotion labels nor support-routing fields are psychological measurements. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-limitations} gives the full statistical, construct, and deployment qualifications.

The engineering tests used controlled inputs and synthetic accounts, and automated accessibility checks do not replace evaluation with assistive-technology users. Most importantly, the small, uncontrolled pilot exercised the deterministic baseline rather than multimodal inference or affect-aware pacing. It can inform workflow refinement but cannot establish adaptation benefit, sustained learning, transfer, or absence of harm. AffectLab is consequently a communication-practice research application, not a diagnostic, clinical, crisis, or therapeutic system.

\section{Prioritised Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion-future-work}

The immediate priority is staging validation with packaged models, live providers, the intended database, networks, and representative concurrency. Service objectives, load and soak tests, failure and backup-restoration exercises, security review, monitoring, and incident exercises should establish operational readiness.

Next, cross-corpus and target-context evaluation should test calibration under language, microphone, noise, transcription, and demographic variation, with repeated seeds and prospective held-out data. Independent annotation should assess feedback errors; psychologically suggestive routing labels need removal, observable-behaviour names, or expert and ethics-led validation. A preregistered baseline-versus-adaptation comparison should measure helpfulness, intrusiveness, task outcomes, and preference without treating engagement as benefit.

Longer-term priorities are participatory accessibility, multilingual and cultural adaptation, and longitudinal retention and transfer studies. These require opt-in controls, data minimisation, transparent uncertainty, a useful baseline, and governed versions of models, prompts, scenarios, thresholds, and safety resources.

\section{Final Conclusion}
\label{sec:final-conclusion}

Contextual and acoustic evidence improved benchmark classification, and AffectLab connected it to structured role-play through bounded, inspectable decisions. This provides an implemented foundation for studying emotion-aware rehearsal with user control. Human benefit and production readiness remain questions for further evidence.
